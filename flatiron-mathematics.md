<p class="mm-lead">My mathematics has the same shape as my Flatiron project: one hidden object, seen only through partial views, made computable by finding the right model. My research asks what the arithmetic of curves and number fields (zeta functions, automorphic forms, special values) looks like inside homotopy theory, especially in algebraic K-theory.</p>

<div class="mm-card" style="background:#fbd3f2">
<h3>The Eisenstein spectrum as an Euler characteristic</h3>
<p>In automorphic forms, the “size” of the Eisenstein spectrum of a curve over a finite field is a ratio of its zeta functions. I show that this ratio is secretly an Euler characteristic in K-theory: an Atiyah–Singer-type formula, with analysis on one side and counting on the other.</p>
</div>

<div class="mm-card" style="background:#d2f2f8">
<h3>New zeta functions in homotopy theory</h3>
<p>With Gabriel Angelini-Knoll, a height-<i>h</i> zeta function built by twisting with the Adams weights on <i>v<sub>h</sub></i>, whose functional equation is a higher arithmetic duality.</p>
</div>

<div class="mm-card" style="background:#fff6b0">
<h3>The Lubin–Tate action</h3>
<p>The symmetries of formal groups behind the stable homotopy groups of spheres, modeled by Hurwitz stacks of curves with a finite group action, and by surgery stacks that reglue those curves at their branch points. At height <i>p</i>−1 the action becomes a symmetry of configurations of <i>p</i>+1 points on P¹ (<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23428">arXiv:2509.23428</a>); the model extends to every height <i>p</i><sup><i>k</i>−1</sup>(<i>p</i>−1) (<i>Only the Gauge Grows</i>, on arXiv soon). With Eva Belmont, this gave the action behind a detection theorem for the <i>p</i> = 3 Kervaire invariant problem.</p>
</div>

With Barthel, Mann, Schlank, Senger, Weinstein and Zhou, I proved the coheight-one weak chromatic splitting conjecture at every height, for generic primes.
